## BioCondition Field Operations Prioritisation Tool

The BioCondition Field Operations Prioritisation Tool is designed to support strategic planning of BioCondition field surveys by identifying Regional Ecosystems (REs) that will provide the greatest return on investment for benchmark development, benchmark supplementation, and Species Benefit Curve (SBC) model improvement. The tool integrates ecosystem extent and decline information, benchmark status, representative site availability, large tree data availability, SBC training data deficiencies, and ecological analogue relationships to generate a prioritisation score for each RE. The outputs are intended for statewide planning, bioregional planning, annual work program development, budget allocation, field survey targeting, and desktop GIS analysis.

The prioritisation workflow combines several datasets. Regional ecosystem attributes are sourced from the Queensland RE mapping products and include ecosystem descriptions, vegetation structure, conservation status, habitat descriptions, protected area occurrence, and mapped pre-clearing and remnant extents. Benchmark information is sourced from the Benchmark Master List and provides benchmark availability, publication status, and reliability ratings. Representative site counts are derived from QBEIS and QBERD datasets and are used to determine whether sufficient reference-condition sites exist to support benchmark development. SBC deficits are sourced from training data assessments and identify ecosystems that remain underrepresented within SBC model calibration datasets. Benchmark analogue relationships are sourced from the ranked RE analogue dataset and are used to estimate how much broader benefit the collection of benchmark data in one ecosystem may provide to similar ecosystems.

The tool first calculates several measures of ecosystem importance and data deficiency. Preclear_ha represents the historical mapped extent of the ecosystem before clearing and is used as a measure of ecosystem importance. Remnant_ha represents the remaining mapped extent and is used as an indicator of current representation and field opportunity. Difference_ha is the amount of clearing that has occurred and is converted into a proportional decline metric that reflects the degree of vegetation loss relative to the original extent. Together, these metrics ensure that both ecosystem importance and threat are incorporated into the prioritisation framework.

Representative site availability is calculated from QBEIS and QBERD. For non-wooded ecosystems, including grasslands, herblands, forblands, bare communities, and sedgelands, benchmark development is based on standard representative sites and is summarised in the standard_sites field. For wooded ecosystems, benchmark development requires the availability of large tree measurements, so benchmark assessment is based on lt_sites, which represents the number of representative sites that contain suitable large tree data. The benchmark_sites field represents the number of sites considered available for benchmark generation after applying these ecosystem-specific rules.

Benchmark status is tracked via the status field in the Benchmark Master List, with values including draft, final unpublished, and final published. Benchmarks with a status of draft are treated as not yet available for prioritisation purposes — their BM flag is effectively set to N so that the RE remains eligible for scoring on benchmark site deficit and SBC deficiency in the same way as an RE without any benchmark.

Benchmark deficits are measured using benchmark_deficit, which represents the number of additional sites required to achieve the benchmark target of three representative sites per ecosystem. A value of zero indicates that sufficient data already exist to generate a benchmark. For wooded ecosystems, lt_deficit represents the number of additional large tree sites required to achieve benchmark requirements. This metric is particularly useful for identifying ecosystems where benchmark development could be completed through revisits or supplementation of existing sites rather than through establishment of entirely new monitoring sites.

SBC model deficiencies are represented by sbc_deficit, which is derived from TD_ref_combined_deficit in the SBC training data assessment. Positive values indicate ecosystems that are underrepresented within SBC model calibration datasets and therefore require additional training data. Values of zero or less indicate that sufficient SBC calibration data already exist and therefore do not contribute to prioritisation. The SBC deficit is also forced to zero for any RE with more than 10 benchmark-quality sites (benchmark_sites > 10), as these ecosystems already have abundant field data and the apparent deficit is not a genuine training gap. Additionally, RE 11.9.5 has its SBC deficit explicitly forced to 0, as its reported deficit of 5 is an artefact of the eastern/western benchmark variant split rather than a genuine training data gap.

To improve dataset alignment, RE codes are cleaned before the benchmark reliability join. Compound RE codes containing a forward slash (e.g. 11.3.25/11.3.1) are resolved to the first code only. The codes 11.9.5_eastern and 11.9.5_western are mapped to the base RE 11.9.5 for reliability lookup, matching the naming convention used in the automisation reliability dataset.

Benchmark requests are sourced from the BM_requests_2526 dataset and represent the count of pending benchmark development requests for each RE for the 2025–26 operational cycle. The request counts are joined to the benchmark selection table as the bm_requests_2526 field and contribute to the need score with a weight of 0.125. This ensures that operational demand signals are reflected alongside ecological urgency in the prioritisation ranking.

Benchmark analogue relationships are incorporated through analogue_influence. Analogue relationships are ranked according to their ecological similarity, with rank_1 analogues assigned a weight of 8, rank_2 analogues assigned a weight of 4, rank_3 analogues assigned a weight of 2, and rank_4 analogues assigned a weight of 1. These weights are summed to generate an analogue influence score for each ecosystem. Ecosystems with high analogue influence are considered capable of generating benchmark information that may support the development of multiple benchmark descriptions, thereby increasing the return on investment from field data collection.

The prioritisation framework is based on two components: a Need Score and a Return Score. The Need Score measures ecological and data collection urgency and is calculated using benchmark deficits, benchmark reliability, proportional decline, historical extent, remnant extent, SBC deficits, and benchmark requests. The current weighting allocates 25.0% to preclear extent, 21.9% to benchmark site number deficits, 15.6% to proportional vegetation decline, 15.6% to benchmark requests, 12.5% to benchmark reliability, 6.3% to remnant extent, and 3.1% to SBC deficits. These weights sum to 0.800 within the need_score additive formula and are expressed as proportions of that total. The reliability term is conditionally applied: valid REs missing from the reliability dataset receive a score of 0 (maximising priority contribution), while non-standard RE codes (not found in the regional ecosystem reference table) have the reliability term dropped entirely from their need_score calculation. The Return Score is based on analogue influence and represents the potential for benchmark information collected in a particular ecosystem to support multiple analogous ecosystems. The final priority_score is calculated by multiplying the Need Score by an analogue influence multiplier, increasing the relative priority of ecosystems that provide broader benchmark benefits.

The prioritisation_flag field provides a plain-language categorisation of each ecosystem. "Benchmark available" indicates that a benchmark already exists for the ecosystem. "Draft benchmark available" indicates that a benchmark record exists but is in draft status and has not yet been finalised — these REs are treated as not yet having a usable benchmark for prioritisation purposes. "Benchmark possible" indicates that sufficient benchmark sites exist to develop a benchmark but that a benchmark has not yet been produced. "Supplementary LT data required" applies to wooded ecosystems where sufficient representative sites are available but additional large tree measurements are required before benchmark development can proceed. "Data required" identifies ecosystems where additional field surveys are still required. If an ecosystem contains an SBC modelling deficiency, the suffix "- SBC gap" is appended to the flag. Examples include "Benchmark available - SBC gap", "Benchmark possible - SBC gap", "Supplementary LT data required - SBC gap", and "Data required - SBC gap". These flags provide a rapid indication of the type of field effort required for each ecosystem.

Several prioritisation outputs are generated for desktop GIS planning. The priority_score is a continuous prioritisation metric where higher values indicate greater expected value from additional field investment. The priority_rank field represents statewide ranking, where a value of 1 indicates the highest statewide priority. The priority_decile field divides prioritised ecosystems into ten statewide priority classes, with Decile 1 representing the highest-priority 10% of ecosystems and Decile 10 representing the lowest-priority 10%. Note that bare ecosystems (STRUCTURE_CODE = Bare) are capped at decile 10 in both statewide and bioregional rankings, reflecting their low priority for BioCondition field operations. The priority_tier field groups deciles into five categories: Very High (Deciles 1–2), High (Deciles 3–4), Medium (Deciles 5–6), Low (Deciles 7–8), and Very Low (Deciles 9–10).

Additional bioregional prioritisation outputs are generated using the same priority score. The priority_rank_BR field represents the priority rank of an ecosystem within its own bioregion. The priority_decile_BR field ranks ecosystems into deciles within each bioregion, allowing priorities to be interpreted relative to local opportunities rather than statewide patterns. The priority_tier_BR field groups bioregional deciles into Very High, High, Medium, Low, and Very Low categories. These regional metrics are particularly useful when field budgets or operational constraints require prioritisation of survey effort within a specific bioregion.

The output GeoPackages contain all prioritisation attributes and are intended for use in QGIS and ArcGIS. Typical GIS workflows involve filtering ecosystems based on prioritisation_flag, symbolising layers using priority_tier or priority_tier_BR, and overlaying prioritisation outputs with tenure, roads, protected areas, existing benchmark sites, SBC sampling locations, and other logistical considerations. High and Very High priority ecosystems can then be grouped spatially to identify efficient field campaigns and maximise data collection outcomes. The combination of benchmark deficits, SBC gaps, analogue influence, statewide priority rankings, and bioregional rankings provides a transparent and repeatable framework for directing BioCondition field investment toward the ecosystems that deliver the greatest strategic value.
